# Chapter 7: Clinical Diagnosis, Biomarkers, and Differential Workup

Diagnosing Fluoroquinolone-Associated Disability has historically been a clinical challenge because routine hospital laboratory panels—such as complete blood counts (CBC), basic metabolic panels, erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR), C-reactive protein (CRP), and standard large-fiber nerve conduction studies—typically return completely normal results. Because the injury occurs at the intracellular and ultrastructural levels (mitochondrial respiratory complexes, mtDNA copy number, unmyelinated C-fibers, and micro-fibrillar collagen cross-linking), clinicians unfamiliar with FQAD frequently misinterpret normal routine labs as evidence of a psychosomatic disorder. Fortunately, formalized diagnostic criteria (**FDA, 2015**; **EMA, 2018**; **Pieper, 2020/2021**; **Cannizzaro & Bennett, 2021**), unsupervised machine-learning validation (**Chalabianloo et al., 2026**), and the **October 2025 ICD-10-CM coding update** now equip physicians with an objective, structured diagnostic framework.

## 1. Established Clinical Diagnostic Criteria

Three complementary clinical frameworks guide the formal diagnosis of FQAD:

First, the **U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) Operational Definition (2015/2016)** defines FQAD by four core requirements:

1.  **Documented Exposure:** Administration of a fluoroquinolone antibiotic (systemic oral or intravenous, or in sensitized individuals, topical otic/ophthalmic formulations).
2.  **Multisystem Involvement:** Adverse events arising in **two or more** of the following six body systems: musculoskeletal (tendon, muscle, joint), peripheral nervous system, neuropsychiatric/central nervous system, special senses (vision, hearing), cardiovascular, and skin.
3.  **Chronicity:** Persistence of disabling symptoms for **30 days or longer** after discontinuation of the fluoroquinolone (whereas the **European Medicines Agency [EMA]** operationalizes long-lasting disability at **90 days or longer**).
4.  **Functional Impairment:** Substantial limitation in performing normal daily life or occupational activities in a patient who was previously able to perform them.

Second, **Dr. Stefan Pieper's Clinical Diagnostic Framework** (*Fluoroquinolone-Associated Disability [FQAD]: Pathogenesis, Diagnostics, Therapy and Diagnostic Criteria*, Springer, 2020/2021) refines this operational definition for practicing clinicians by incorporating the characteristic **temporal window of onset** (symptoms beginning during treatment or with a delayed latency of up to **6 months** post-cessation due to mtDNA turnover dilution), the characteristic **relapsing-remitting ("wave") trajectory**, and the exclusion of alternative primary diagnoses.

Third, **Dr. Charles L. Bennett and the SONAR Network** (**Cannizzaro et al., 2021, *Medicina***; validated experimentally by **Getaneh et al., 2025, *Pharmaceuticals***) demonstrated that **Functional Gastrointestinal Disorders (FGIDs)**—affecting roughly 70% of FQAD patients via vagal and enteric GABA-A disruption—should be formally included as an additional diagnostic domain alongside the classic musculoskeletal, neurological, and neuropsychiatric criteria. Furthermore, **Chalabianloo et al. (2026, *British Journal of Clinical Pharmacology*)** demonstrated through unsupervised machine learning across 40,127 FAERS reports that the multi-system co-occurrence of neuropsychiatric (memory impairment, anxiety, insomnia), musculoskeletal (tendinopathy, muscle atrophy), and sensory/ocular symptoms forms a statistically distinct, objective clinical cluster independent of observer bias.

## 2. The October 2025 Breakthrough: ICD-10-CM T36.AX Diagnosis Codes

On **October 1, 2025** (FY 2026 ICD-10-CM implementation), a historic regulatory milestone ended the era of diagnostic invisibility for fluoroquinolone-injured patients. The U.S. Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS) and National Center for Health Statistics (NCHS) established the **T36.AX** subcategory specifically for *"Poisoning by, adverse effect of, and underdosing of fluoroquinolones."*

For patients who developed toxicity from properly prescribed therapeutic doses, clinicians should record the **Adverse Effect** codes paired with the appropriate 7th-character encounter extension:

*   **T36.AX5A — Adverse effect of fluoroquinolones, initial encounter:** Used while the patient is receiving active evaluation or acute medical treatment for a newly diagnosed fluoroquinolone adverse reaction.
*   **T36.AX5D — Adverse effect of fluoroquinolones, subsequent encounter:** Used during routine follow-up visits and ongoing recovery care after the initial acute phase.
*   **T36.AX5S — Adverse effect of fluoroquinolones, sequela:** Used specifically to document **chronic, long-term, or residual disabling conditions (FQAD / FQMT)** that persist months or years after the acute drug exposure has ended.

In standard ICD-10-CM coding practice, the **T36.AX5-** etiology code is paired with specific **organ-manifestation codes** that capture the patient's active clinical domains, such as:

*   **G62.0:** Drug-induced polyneuropathy (or **G60.8** for small fiber neuropathy).
*   **M76.60 / M76.61 / M76.62:** Achilles tendinitis (unspecified, right, or left) or **M66.2- / M66.3-** for spontaneous tendon rupture.
*   **G90.8 / G90.9:** Other / unspecified disorder of the autonomic nervous system (or **G90.A** for Postural Orthostatic Tachycardia Syndrome [POTS]).
*   **E88.40:** Mitochondrial metabolism disorder, unspecified.
*   **F06.8 / F41.0:** Other specified mental disorders due to known physiological condition / panic disorder.

Documenting these exact codes in the electronic health record validates the organic nature of the syndrome, prevents inappropriate re-prescribing of fluoroquinolones across hospital networks, and provides the objective medical coding required for short- and long-term disability claims.

## 3. Objective Diagnostic Testing: Seeing the "Invisible" Injury

Because standard screening tests are insensitive to intracellular and ultrastructural damage, targeted diagnostic modalities should be selected based on the patient's predominant symptoms:

### Neurological and Autonomic Assessment
When standard electromyography (EMG) and nerve conduction velocity (NCV) studies are normal despite severe burning, tingling, or electric-shock pain, the gold-standard objective test is a **3-mm Skin Punch Biopsy** (typically taken from the distal leg 10 cm above the lateral malleolus and the proximal thigh) immunostained with the pan-axonal marker **PGP 9.5** to quantify **Intraepidermal Nerve Fiber Density (IENFD)** and sweat gland innervation. An IENFD below the 5th percentile for age and sex provides definitive histological proof of **Small Fiber Neuropathy (SFN)**. Non-invasive **In Vivo Corneal Confocal Microscopy (CCM)** can also quantify small-fiber nerve density and tortuosity in the corneal sub-basal plexus. For autonomic dysfunction, a **Tilt-Table Test** combined with the **Quantitative Sudomotor Axon Reflex Test (QSART)** and heart rate variability (HRV) testing objectively documents POTS and postganglionic sudomotor denervation.

### Musculoskeletal and Connective Tissue Imaging
As highlighted in the **2025** clinical report by **Piazza, Weiss, and Rosemann (*Praxis*)** and **Moseley et al. (2025, *Cureus*)**, conventional 1.5-Tesla **Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI)** frequently shows only minimal peritendinitis or appears deceptively normal in FQAD patients suffering from severe, disabling tendon pain. This occurs because MRI detects macroscopic fluid edema and gross tears, whereas early fluoroquinolone injury involves **nanoscale collagen fibril thinning, loss of LOX cross-linking, and reduced fibril stiffness** (**Anand et al., 2026**). **High-resolution Musculoskeletal Ultrasound (MSUS)** with Power Doppler and **Shear-Wave Elastography (SWE)** is often superior for dynamic bed-side evaluation: as demonstrated in **Mișcă et al.'s (2025)** clinical trial, ultrasound detects subtle tendon thickening, hypoechoic micro-fissuring, and pathological neovascularization, while shear-wave elastography directly measures the loss of tendon mechanical stiffness.

### Cardiovascular and Ophthalmic Screening
Any patient with chest, back, or interscapular pain, a history of hypertension, or features of a connective tissue disorder should undergo **Transthoracic Echocardiography (TTE)** (specifically measuring aortic root and ascending aorta diameters and assessing mitral/aortic valve competence) or **CT/MR Angiography**, alongside a **12-lead ECG** to evaluate the QTc interval. Patients who took moxifloxacin or who experience photophobia, floaters, or visual changes should receive a comprehensive slit-lamp examination checking specifically for **iris transillumination defects and pigment dispersion** (**Chau & Au, 2026**), intraocular pressure measurement, and dilated fundus exam.

### Cellular, Mitochondrial, and Redox Biomarkers
Specialized functional and biochemical panels used by integrative and mitochondrial specialists (**Pieper, 2021**; **Michalak et al., 2017**) can help quantify metabolic bottlenecks:

*   **Intracellular Cation Status:** **Red Blood Cell (RBC) Magnesium**, **RBC Zinc**, and **Serum Copper / Ceruloplasmin** (avoiding reliance on serum magnesium alone, which represents less than 1% of total body magnesium and is homeostatically maintained at the expense of intracellular stores).
*   **Oxidative and Nitrosative Stress Markers:** Urinary or serum **8-hydroxy-2'-deoxyguanosine (8-OHdG)** (a direct marker of oxidative guanosine cleavage in mtDNA and nuclear DNA), **Malondialdehyde (MDA)** or **F2-isoprostanes** (lipid peroxidation of mitochondrial membranes), and **3-Nitrotyrosine** (footprint of peroxynitrite [ONOO⁻] protein nitration).
*   **Bioenergetic and Mitochondrial Markers:** Fasting **Lactate-to-Pyruvate ratio** (elevated when Complex I/IV or Pyruvate Dehydrogenase is blocked), plasma **Coenzyme Q10 (ubiquinol/ubiquinone)**, **Acylcarnitine profile**, and urinary **Organic Acids Testing (OAT)** (assessing Krebs cycle intermediates such as citrate, isocitrate, α-ketoglutarate, succinate, fumarate, and malate, which reflect bottlenecks at aconitase and IDH2).

## 4. Differential Diagnosis: Ruling Out Mimics and Unmasked Co-Morbidities

A thorough diagnostic workup must simultaneously rule out independent "mimics" and identify pre-existing conditions that may have been unmasked or exacerbated by the fluoroquinolone insult:

*   **Systemic Autoimmune and Rheumatologic Diseases:** Antinuclear antibodies (ANA), anti-dsDNA, extractable nuclear antigens (SSA/Ro, SSB/La for Sjögren's syndrome, which also causes SFN and dry eyes), rheumatoid factor (RF), anti-CCP, and HLA-B27 (for spondyloarthropathies/enthesitis) should be checked.
*   **Nutritional Neuropathies and the Vitamin B6 Trap:** Clinicians must check **Vitamin B12 (cobalamin)** along with **methylmalonic acid (MMA)** and **homocysteine**, **Folate**, **25-OH Vitamin D**, and critically, **Serum Vitamin B6 (pyridoxal 5'-phosphate)**. Both Vitamin B6 deficiency and **Vitamin B6 toxicity** (often from over-the-counter neuro-supplements or energy drinks) cause a severe sensory dorsal root ganglionopathy that closely mimics or amplifies FQAD neuropathy.
*   **Endocrine and Infectious Mimics:** Thyroid panel (TSH, free T4, free T3, anti-TPO), HbA1c and fasting insulin, and where clinically indicated by exposure history, *Borrelia burgdorferi* (Lyme disease) and reactivated viral serologies (EBV, CMV, HHV-6).
*   **Underlying Heritable Connective Tissue Disorders:** Because fluoroquinolones violently unmask subclinical collagen and fibrillin defects (**LeMaire et al., 2022, 2026**), patients presenting with widespread joint hypermobility, translucent skin, easy bruising, or arterial tortuosity should be clinically evaluated (using the Beighton score and genetic panels where appropriate) for **Hypermobile Ehlers-Danlos Syndrome (hEDS)**, **Classical or Vascular EDS (COL5A1, COL3A1)**, or **Marfan/Loeys-Dietz spectrum disorders**.
