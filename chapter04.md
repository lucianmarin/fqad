# Chapter 4: Connective Tissue, Musculoskeletal, and Vascular Pathobiology

Fluoroquinolones are uniquely destructive to human connective tissues. Whereas most adverse drug reactions affecting the musculoskeletal system cause transient inflammatory arthralgia or myalgia, fluoroquinolones directly dismantle the structural architecture of the **extracellular matrix (ECM)** across tendons, ligaments, entheses, articular cartilage, skeletal muscle myotendinous junctions, and the elastic media of the arterial wall. As **Sewell and Jensen (2026)** note, musculoskeletal and tendon injury is the prototypical and best-characterized manifestation of fluoroquinolone-associated multisystem toxicity—yet its molecular mechanisms have only recently been unified through advances in tenocyte biology (**Anand et al., 2026**; **Romanowska et al., 2025**; **Millar et al.**) and vascular pathobiology (**LeMaire & Shen, 2018, 2022, 2026**; **Lv et al., 2026**).

## 1. Carrier-Mediated Tenocyte Uptake and Collagen Type I Sabotage (2025–2026 Insights)

Tendons transmit immense mechanical forces from muscle to bone through a hierarchical matrix composed primarily of **Type I collagen** (making up 70–80% of tendon dry weight), elastin, proteoglycans (decorin, biglycan, aggrecan), and glycoproteins such as **fibronectin**. This dense matrix is maintained by specialized resident fibroblasts called **tenocytes**, which operate in a relatively hypovascular, low-oxygen environment—most notably in the "watershed zone" of the Achilles tendon, located 2 to 6 centimeters proximal to its calcaneal insertion.

In **May 2026**, **Anand, Sakai, Dickens, and colleagues** published a landmark mechanistic study in *Scientific Reports* (*"Effects of fluoroquinolone antibiotics on extracellular matrix-related phenotypes in tendon cells"*), complemented by **Romanowska, Adamus, et al. (2025, *International Journal of Molecular Sciences*)**. Together with **Badal et al. (2015, *JBC*)**, these studies map a multi-step cascade of collagen sabotage inside the tenocyte:

1.  **Active Intracellular Accumulation:** Anand et al. (2026) demonstrated that ciprofloxacin does not merely diffuse passively around tendons; it undergoes **carrier-mediated active intracellular uptake** directly into tendon cells, concentrating inside the tenocyte cytoplasm and mitochondria.
2.  **Suppression of Type I Collagen and Prolyl 4-Hydroxylation:** Once inside the tenocyte, fluoroquinolones downregulate both the synthesis and the structural assembly of **Type I collagen**. By chelating catalytic Fe²⁺ from **collagen prolyl 4-hydroxylases (P4H)** (**Badal et al., 2015**), ciprofloxacin significantly reduces the **hydroxyproline content** of nascent procollagen chains (**Anand et al., 2026**). Because 4-hydroxyproline residues are required to form intramolecular hydrogen bonds that stabilize the collagen triple helix at physiological temperatures (37°C), under-hydroxylated collagen fibers are thermally unstable, misfolded, and rapidly degraded.
3.  **Inhibition of Lysyl Oxidase (LOX) Cross-Linking:** Even after tropocollagen molecules are secreted into the extracellular space, they possess very little tensile strength until they are covalently cross-linked into mature fibrils by **Lysyl Oxidase (LOX)**, a **copper (Cu²⁺)-dependent** amine oxidase that converts lysine and hydroxylysine residues into reactive aldehydes (allysine). Both **LeMaire et al. (2018, 2026)** and **Anand et al. (2026)** proved that ciprofloxacin suppresses LOX expression and enzymatic activity. As a result, collagen fibrils formed during or after fluoroquinolone exposure have significantly **reduced fibril diameter and mechanical stiffness** (**Anand et al., 2026**), rendering the tendon brittle and susceptible to micro-fissuring and spontaneous rupture under ordinary physiological loads.
4.  **Elimination of HIF-1α Adaptive Signaling:** Because tendons are poorly vascularized, tenocytes rely on **HIF-1α** to orchestrate anaerobic survival and angiogenic repair. By blocking HIF-1α mRNA translation (**Badal et al., 2015**), fluoroquinolones starve the watershed zone of its primary repair signal.

## 2. Integrin Deprivation, Fibronectin Loss, and Tenocyte Anoikis

A healthy tenocyte is continuously mechanosensitive: it grips the surrounding collagen and fibronectin fibers using transmembrane **β1-integrin** receptors (α1β1, α2β1, and α5β1). When mechanical tension is transmitted across β1-integrins, intracellular **Focal Adhesion Kinase (FAK)** and **Src** are phosphorylated, transmitting pro-survival signals to the nucleus and instructing the tenocyte to synthesize reparative extracellular matrix.

Fluoroquinolones sever this vital cell–matrix lifeline through two converging mechanisms demonstrated by **Sendzik et al.** and confirmed in **2026 by Anand et al. (*Scientific Reports*)**:

*   **Cation Stripping from the Integrin MIDAS Site:** Integrin receptors require divalent cations (Mg²⁺, Ca²⁺, or Mn²⁺) bound at their extracellular **Metal Ion-Dependent Adhesion Site (MIDAS)** to maintain their active, high-affinity ligand-binding conformation. Fluoroquinolone chelation of local Mg²⁺ and Mn²⁺ collapses β1-integrin into its inactive state.
*   **Downregulation of Fibronectin and Active β1-Integrin:** Anand et al. (2026) showed that ciprofloxacin directly reduces tenocyte production of **fibronectin**—the adhesive glycoprotein bridge of the ECM—and markedly decreases cell-surface expression of **active β1-integrin**, accompanied by reduced FAK phosphorylation.

When a tenocyte loses its integrin-mediated attachment to the extracellular matrix, it undergoes a specific form of anchorage-dependent programmed cell death called **anoikis** (from the Greek for "homelessness"). Because tendons already have a low baseline cellularity (tenocytes comprise less than 5% of tendon volume), tenocyte anoikis and apoptosis leave behind acellular, necrotic zones that lack the resident cells required to rebuild the damaged matrix.

## 3. The cGAS–STING Pathway and the Matrix Metalloproteinase (MMP) Catabolic Storm

Simultaneously with the arrest of collagen synthesis and cross-linking, fluoroquinolones unleash an unopposed catabolic storm of **Matrix Metalloproteinases (MMPs)**—the zinc-dependent endopeptidases that digest extracellular matrix proteins. Across human and animal studies, ciprofloxacin and levofloxacin drive dramatic upregulation of **MMP-1 (interstitial collagenase)** and **MMP-13 (collagenase-3)**, which cleave the native triple helix of Type I and Type III collagen, alongside **MMP-2 (gelatinase A)** and **MMP-9 (gelatinase B)**, which degrade denatured collagen fragments and elastin fibers. At the same time, expression of endogenous **Tissue Inhibitors of Metalloproteinases (TIMP-1 and TIMP-2)** is suppressed, tipping the MMP/TIMP ratio into runaway proteolysis.

How does a topoisomerase-inhibiting antibiotic trigger massive MMP gene transcription? In addition to ROS-mediated activation of **Nuclear Factor kappa B (NF-κB)** and **Activator Protein-1 (AP-1)**, **Dr. Scott A. LeMaire, Dr. Ying H. Shen, and colleagues** at Baylor College of Medicine uncovered a crucial upstream trigger in their landmark **2018 *JAMA Surgery*** study. They demonstrated that when ciprofloxacin damages mitochondrial and nuclear DNA in smooth muscle and connective tissue cells, fragments of damaged **mtDNA and nuclear DNA leak into the cytoplasm**.

Once in the cytosol, this ectopic self-DNA is detected as a danger signal by the innate immune sensor **cGAS (cyclic GMP-AMP synthase)**, which activates **STING (Stimulator of Interferon Genes)** on the endoplasmic reticulum. Activated STING phosphorylates **TBK1** and **IRF3/NF-κB**, which translocate to the nucleus and directly drive the **overexpression of MMP-2 and MMP-9** while **suppressing Lysyl Oxidase (LOX)**! When LeMaire's team genetically silenced STING, ciprofloxacin-induced LOX suppression and MMP upregulation were markedly attenuated—proving that **mitochondrial DNA damage and cytosolic DNA sensing via the cGAS–STING pathway directly orchestrate extracellular matrix destruction**.

Furthermore, as tendinopathy specialist **Dr. Neal L. Millar** (University of Glasgow) and colleagues have shown in human tendinopathy mechanobiology, oxidative stress and nitric oxide/cytokine signaling cause tenocytes to switch from producing strong, thick, load-bearing **Type I collagen** to weak, thin, haphazardly arranged **Type III collagen**—the biological equivalent of patching a suspension bridge cable with twine.

## 4. Beyond the Achilles: Pan-Tendinopathy, Myotendinous Strains, and Cartilage Toxicity

Although roughly 85–90% of reported fluoroquinolone tendon ruptures involve the **Achilles tendon** (and up to half occur bilaterally), FQAD frequently manifests as a **pan-tendinopathy** and **enthesopathy** affecting tendons and fascia throughout the body: the patellar and quadriceps tendons, the gluteal and hamstring tendons, the plantar fascia, the rotator cuff (supraspinatus), the lateral and medial epicondyles of the elbow, the flexor and extensor tendons of the hands and wrists, and the temporomandibular joint (TMJ).

Recent clinical literature highlights two important clinical nuances:

*   **Myotendinous Junction Injury Without Classic Rupture:** As documented by **Moseley, Cluney, and Dennis (2025, *Cureus*)**, fluoroquinolone injury does not always present as a clean mid-substance Achilles tear visible on routine MRI; it can manifest months after ciprofloxacin or levofloxacin exposure as recurrent, debilitating **myotendinous junction strains** (such as at the gastrocnemius–soleus junction) triggered by minimal daily activity.
*   **Hormonal Co-Risk with Testosterone Therapy:** In **August 2026**, **Farzam (*Cureus*)** reported rapid-onset severe bilateral Achilles tendinopathy in a young adult male on physiologic **testosterone replacement therapy (TRT)** shortly after starting ciprofloxacin. Because exogenous androgens increase skeletal muscle force generation while independently downregulating tendon relaxin/MMP remodeling balance, combining TRT or anabolic steroids with fluoroquinolones creates a high-risk biomechanical mismatch—paralleling the well-known **3- to 6-fold increased rupture risk** seen when fluoroquinolones are combined with **corticosteroids**.

In articular cartilage, fluoroquinolones accumulate heavily and chelate Mg²⁺ from chondrocyte β1-integrins, inducing chondrocyte apoptosis, proteoglycan depletion, and matrix blistering. While early arthropathy warnings focused on weight-bearing joints in juvenile animals, **Kitano and Yoshida (2026, *International Journal of Infectious Diseases*)** analyzed nearly one million propensity-score-matched children (<18 years) in the US TriNetX database, while adult FQAD cohorts consistently report widespread joint crepitus, cartilage thinning, and accelerated secondary osteoarthritis.

## 5. Vascular Integrity: Aortic Aneurysm, Dissection, Heritable Connective Tissue Disorders, and the Gut–Vascular Axis

The tunica media of the human **aorta** relies on concentric lamellae of **Type I and Type III collagen** and **elastin**, cross-linked by **Lysyl Oxidase (LOX)** and maintained by vascular smooth muscle cells (VSMCs), to withstand a lifetime of pulsatile systolic blood pressure. Because the aortic wall shares the exact structural biochemistry of tendons, the same cGAS–STING, LOX-suppression, and MMP-2/MMP-9 cascade that ruptures the Achilles tendon can weaken the aortic media, leading to **aortic aneurysm (AA)** or catastrophic **aortic dissection (AD)** and rupture (**Narita et al., 2026**).

The experimental and epidemiological evidence through **2025–2026** has established three critical pillars regarding fluoroquinolone vascular toxicity:

1.  **Extreme Danger in Heritable Connective Tissue Disorders (Marfan and vEDS):** Following their 2018 *JAMA Surgery* sporadic AAD mouse study, **Dr. Scott A. LeMaire's group** demonstrated in **2022 (*Journal of Thoracic and Cardiovascular Surgery*)** that ciprofloxacin administration to **Marfan syndrome (*Fbn1* C1041G/+)** mice significantly accelerated aortic root dilation, nearly doubled aortic dissection rates (47% vs. 25%), and quintupled fatal aortic rupture (25% vs. 5%). In **2026 (*Annals of Surgery*)**, **LeMaire, Zhang, et al.** tested ciprofloxacin in a mouse model of **vascular Ehlers-Danlos syndrome (vEDS, *Col3a1* G209S/WT)**. Whereas 100% of vehicle-treated vEDS mice survived the study period, **44% of ciprofloxacin-treated vEDS mice died of fatal aortic or arterial rupture** (presenting as hemopericardium or hemothorax, most within 7 days of exposure), with aortic tissues showing severe LOX depletion, MMP-2/MMP-9 elevation, macrophage infiltration, and VSMC apoptosis. These findings establish an absolute clinical imperative: **fluoroquinolones must be strictly avoided in patients with Marfan syndrome, Loeys-Dietz syndrome, Ehlers-Danlos syndrome (especially vEDS, cEDS, and hEDS), bicuspid aortic valve, or known aortic dilatation**.
2.  **The Gut–Vascular Axis in Aortic Dissection (2026):** In a novel **2026** study published in *Cardiovascular Toxicology*, **Lv, Wang, Abudesimu, and colleagues** demonstrated that ciprofloxacin aggravates aortic dissection not only via direct VSMC toxicity, but also through a **gut–vascular axis**. By decimating commensal gut microbiota and impairing intestinal tight-junction barrier integrity, ciprofloxacin caused translocation of bacterial **lipopolysaccharide (LPS / endotoxin)** into the systemic circulation. In human aortic smooth muscle cells (HASMCs), circulating LPS synergized with ciprofloxacin to accelerate VSMC phenotypic switching and apoptosis—linking antibiotic-induced gut dysbiosis directly to vascular wall vulnerability.
3.  **Population Epidemiology (2025–2026):** In **2025**, **Rosenbusch, Below, Klimke, and Bätzing (*BMC Cardiovascular Disorders*)** analyzed nationwide German statutory health insurance claims covering ~87% of the German population and confirmed that fluoroquinolone exposure is associated with a statistically significant increased risk of aortic aneurysm and dissection compared to non-fluoroquinolone antibiotics (**HR 1.56**; and **HR 1.48** after rigorous adjustment for underlying infection severity, age, and sex). Similarly, a **2026** U.S. multicenter propensity-score-matched cohort study of 361,872 patients by **Chang, Chen, Wang, et al. (*Journal of Microbiology, Immunology and Infection*)** found that while overall 90-day incident aneurysm diagnosis was neutral (aHR 0.95), fluoroquinolone exposure was associated with significantly elevated 90-day risks of **acute aortic dissection (aHR 1.38)** and **aneurysm-related surgery (aHR 2.32)**.
