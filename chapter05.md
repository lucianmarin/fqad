# Chapter 5: Central, Peripheral, and Autonomic Neurotoxicity

The neurological and neuropsychiatric manifestations of Fluoroquinolone-Associated Disability are among the most distressing and life-altering features of the syndrome. Because fluoroquinolones are lipophilic, fluorinated molecules that readily cross both the blood-brain barrier (BBB) and the blood-nerve barrier (BNB), they gain direct access to central neurons, glial cells, dorsal root ganglia, unmyelinated peripheral C-fibers, and the autonomic and enteric ganglia. Between **2025 and 2026**, large-scale active-comparator pharmacovigilance and cohort studies (**Brown et al., 2026, *Neurology***; **Guo et al., 2026**; **Shamim et al., 2026**; **Raguram et al., 2026**; **Chau & Au, 2026**; **Omrani et al., 2025**; **Kitano & Yoshida, 2026**) and animal models (**Getaneh et al., 2025**) have quantified the magnitude of these neurotoxic effects and illuminated their underlying mechanisms.

## 1. The GABA/Glutamate Excitotoxic Shift and Acute Neuropsychiatric Syndromes

Healthy central nervous system function depends on a finely tuned equilibrium between synaptic **inhibition**, mediated primarily by **gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA)** acting on chloride-conducting **GABA-A receptors**, and synaptic **excitation**, mediated by **L-glutamate** acting on **NMDA** and **AMPA** receptors. Fluoroquinolones destabilize both sides of this seesaw simultaneously, driving the brain into acute **excitotoxicity**.

First, fluoroquinolones act as selective competitive and non-competitive **GABA-A receptor antagonists**. The nitrogenous heterocyclic substituent at the **C-7 position**—particularly the unsubstituted piperazine ring of **ciprofloxacin** and **norfloxacin**, or the bulky pyrrolidine/methylpiperazine rings of **moxifloxacin** and **levofloxacin**—shares structural homology with GABA and binds directly to the GABA-A receptor complex, preventing GABA from opening the chloride channel. Without inward chloride (Cl⁻) current to hyperpolarize the postsynaptic membrane, the brain's inhibitory "brakes" are effectively severed. Crucially, if a patient takes a **non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID)** such as ibuprofen, naproxen, diclofenac, or ketorolac—or methylxanthines such as **theophylline** or high-dose caffeine—alongside a fluoroquinolone, the binding affinity of the fluoroquinolone for the GABA-A receptor increases dramatically, lowering the seizure threshold and triggering severe central nervous system hyperexcitability.

Second, fluoroquinolones provoke **NMDA Receptor Hyperactivation**. Under resting physiological conditions, the ion pore of the **N-methyl-D-aspartate (NMDA)** glutamate receptor is physically plugged by an extracellular **magnesium ion (Mg²⁺)**, preventing calcium influx until the neuron is strongly depolarized. Because fluoroquinolones chelate local Mg²⁺ ions—and because GABA-A disinhibition chronically depolarizes the neuronal membrane—this protective Mg²⁺ block is expelled from the NMDA channel pore. Calcium (Ca²⁺) floods into the neuron, activating calcium-dependent proteases (**calpains**), phospholipases, endonucleases, and **neuronal nitric oxide synthase (nNOS)**, which floods the cell with nitric oxide and peroxynitrite (ONOO⁻) and triggers mitochondrial permeability transition and neuronal apoptosis.

Clinically, this dual GABA/glutamate catastrophe produces a distinct, chemically induced neuropsychiatric syndrome—often referred to as **"organic brain syndrome" or chemical terror**—even in patients with zero prior psychiatric history. Recent peer-reviewed pharmacovigilance analyses have quantified these risks against active antibiotic comparators:

*   **Panic Attacks (Raguram, Chalabianloo, et al., 2026, *Journal of Antimicrobial Chemotherapy*):** In a systematic review and active-comparator FAERS disproportionality analysis (2004–2024), ciprofloxacin, levofloxacin, and moxifloxacin were associated with a **6-fold increase in reports of panic attacks** compared to azithromycin and a **12-fold increase** compared to trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole, with clinical trial prevalence ranging from 0.46% to 1.76%.
*   **Nightmares and Severe Insomnia (Omrani, Chalabianloo, et al., 2025, *Journal of Psychopharmacology*):** Active-comparator FAERS analysis demonstrated that fluoroquinolone-associated nightmare reports were **6- to 10-fold higher** than those for azithromycin (ROR 6.18) and trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole (ROR 10.38), alongside **Kitano and Yoshida's (2026)** finding of significantly elevated sleep disorders in children (<18 years) exposed to fluoroquinolones.
*   **Akathisia, Depersonalization, Psychosis, and Suicide Vulnerability (Kaur, Bennett, et al., 2016; Chau & Au, 2026, *Pharmacology Research & Perspectives*):** In SONAR's collaborative survey (**Kaur et al., 2016**), 93 of 94 respondents with fluoroquinolone neuropsychiatric toxicity reported anxiety, insomnia, panic attacks, clouded thinking, depersonalization, nightmares, memory impairment, or suicidal thoughts. In **February 2026**, **Chau and Au** analyzed 44,895 FQ adverse event reports in FAERS (2011–2024), identifying 8,518 psychiatric cases. Crucially, while females accounted for the majority of overall psychiatric adverse event reports, systematic characterization of **completed suicide and suicide attempt cases revealed a striking male predominance—identifying middle-aged men as a particularly high-risk demographic** requiring urgent clinical vigilance and early screening.

## 2. Peripheral Neuropathy and Small Fiber Neuropathy (SFN): 2019–2026 Evidence

Peripheral neuropathy (PN) was added as a permanent risk to FDA labels in 2013, and its epidemiology and mechanism have been definitively established through a series of landmark studies culminating in **2026**:

*   **Population Cohort Confirmation (Morales et al., 2019, *JAMA Neurology*; Brown, Wing, Mansfield, et al., 2026, *Neurology*):** Using large UK electronic primary care databases (THIN and CPRD Aurum/GOLD) involving over 1.3 million adult patients without diabetes, **Morales et al. (2019)** and **Brown et al. (2026)** demonstrated that systemic oral fluoroquinolone prescriptions significantly increase the incidence of peripheral neuropathy compared to comparator antibiotics (amoxicillin-clavulanate and cephalosporins), with an adjusted incidence rate ratio of **1.47**. Critically, the risk increased by approximately **3% for each additional day** of fluoroquinolone exposure and remained significantly elevated for up to **180 days** after prescription.
*   **Pediatric Neuropathy Risk (Kitano & Yoshida, 2026, *International Journal of Infectious Diseases*):** In a propensity-score-matched TriNetX analysis of nearly one million U.S. children stratified into ages <5, 5–11, and 12–17 years, fluoroquinolone exposure increased the odds of **peripheral neuropathy by 1.52- to 2.22-fold** (p < 0.001) across all three pediatric age groups compared to macrolides.
*   **Pharmacovigilance and Drug-Interaction Signals (Guo et al., 2026, *International Journal of Antimicrobial Agents*; Shamim, Doughty, Chau, Brown, & Baldock, 2026, *Clinical and Translational Science*):** Analyzing 6,451+ peripheral neuropathy reports across U.S. (FAERS) and Canadian (CVARD) databases through 2025, **Guo et al. (2026)** and **Shamim et al. (2026)** confirmed robust disproportionality signals across ciprofloxacin, levofloxacin, moxifloxacin, ofloxacin, and notably **gemifloxacin**. Onset of neuropathy was characteristically rapid, with a **median time-to-onset of just 4 to 5 days** after starting therapy (though cumulative late-onset neuropathy also occurs, as illustrated by **Prus & Lenox, 2026** in *Cureus* after 17 weeks of moxifloxacin). Crucially, multivariable logistic regression in **Guo et al. (2026)** identified **extended treatment duration** and **concomitant or sequential nitrofurantoin use** (another antibiotic frequently prescribed for UTIs that independently causes axonal neuropathy) as major risk amplifiers.

At the cellular level, fluoroquinolone neuropathy is primarily a **mitochondrial distal axonopathy** with a strong predilection for **Small Fiber Neuropathy (SFN)**. Unmyelinated **C-fibers** and thinly myelinated **Aδ-fibers**—which transmit burning pain, thermal sensation, and autonomic signals to blood vessels and sweat glands—possess extraordinarily high surface-to-volume ratios and lack the energy-saving saltatory conduction of heavily myelinated motor nerves. Their distal axon terminals in the feet and hands depend on a continuous supply of healthy mitochondria transported down microtubules via kinesin motor proteins from the dorsal root ganglion (DRG) soma. When fluoroquinolones deplete mtDNA copy number (**Hangas et al., 2018**) and stall Complexes I and IV (**Reinhardt et al., 2025**), axonal transport fails and the distal nerve endings undergo Wallerian-like **"dying-back" degeneration**. Because routine nerve conduction studies (NCS) and electromyography (EMG) measure only large, fast-conducting myelinated Aα/Aβ fibers, they are completely normal in pure SFN—explaining why standard neurology workups frequently miss FQAD neuropathy unless a **3-mm skin punch biopsy** for Intraepidermal Nerve Fiber Density (IENFD) or **corneal confocal microscopy** is performed.

## 3. Autonomic Dysfunction (Dysautonomia), Vagal Blockade, and the Enteric Nervous System (2021–2025 Updates)

Because small autonomic nerve fibers (postganglionic sympathetic and parasympathetic fibers) and the **vagus nerve (cranial nerve X)** rely on the same mitochondrial bioenergetics and GABA-A receptor signaling, autonomic nervous system dysfunction (**dysautonomia**) is a cardinal feature of FQAD. Patients frequently develop **Postural Orthostatic Tachycardia Syndrome (POTS)**, inappropriate sinus tachycardia, orthostatic hypotension, erratic blood pressure swings, impaired thermoregulation, heat intolerance, and sudomotor (sweating) abnormalities.

Nowhere is this autonomic disruption clearer than in the **vagus nerve and the gastrointestinal tract**. In the brainstem dorsal vagal complex—comprising the nucleus tractus solitarius (NTS) and the dorsal motor nucleus of the vagus (DMV)—as well as within the myenteric and submucosal plexuses of the **enteric nervous system (ENS)**, GABA-A and GABA-B receptors finely regulate vagal efferent tone, esophageal sphincter relaxation, gastric accommodation, and intestinal peristalsis.

In **2021**, **Cannizzaro, Naughton, Freeman, Martin, and Bennett (*Medicina*)** investigated gastrointestinal sequelae in ~300 fluoroquinolone-injured patients alongside FAERS reports and proposed **Functional Gastrointestinal Disorders (FGIDs)** as a major new diagnostic criterion for FQAD. They found that approximately **70% of FQ-injured respondents** scored positive for persistent FGIDs—far beyond simple antibiotic-associated diarrhea—irrespective of which fluoroquinolone was taken or the duration of the course.

In **August 2025**, **Getaneh, Kerler, Ganzorig, Muhl, and Miller** published a landmark experimental validation in *Pharmaceuticals* (*"Fluoroquinolone-Associated Disability: A Rodent Model Reveals Transient Neuropsychiatric and Persistent Gastrointestinal Effects of Low-Dose Ciprofloxacin"*). Administering low-dose oral ciprofloxacin (20 mg/kg/day for 14 days) to Sprague-Dawley rats compared against amoxicillin and saline controls, Getaneh et al. demonstrated two key findings:

1.  Ciprofloxacin induced an acute anxiety-like neurobehavioral phenotype that gradually resolved over four weeks post-treatment in healthy rodents.
2.  Critically, ciprofloxacin induced **persistent gastrointestinal dysmotility—specifically a significant acceleration of GI transit** recovered across the stomach and distal small intestine—that remained significantly altered weeks after ciprofloxacin had been discontinued.

Clinically, this vagal and enteric GABAergic disruption, compounded by mitochondrial injury to the interstitial cells of Cajal (the gut's pacemakers) and microbiome depletion, manifests across a spectrum of chronic FQAD dysmotility: some patients experience persistent **accelerated transit, bile acid malabsorption, and visceral hypersensitivity (IBS-D phenotype)** as seen in the Getaneh et al. (2025) model, while others with vagal efferent denervation develop **delayed gastric emptying (gastroparesis)**, severe bloating, and pseudo-obstruction.

## 4. Microglial Activation, Neuroinflammation, and Environmental Synergy

Beyond direct neuronal and axonal injury, fluoroquinolones prime the innate immune system of the central nervous system. **Microglia**, the resident tissue macrophages of the brain and spinal cord, normally exist in a ramified, neuroprotective surveillance state. When exposed to fluoroquinolone-induced reactive oxygen species, damaged mitochondrial DNA fragments (DAMPs), and excitotoxic glutamate spillover, microglia polarize into an activated, pro-inflammatory **M1 phenotype**.

Activated M1 microglia release pro-inflammatory cytokines—including **Interleukin-1β (IL-1β)**, **Tumor Necrosis Factor-alpha (TNF-α)**, and **Interleukin-6 (IL-6)**—along with inducible nitric oxide synthase (iNOS)-derived nitric oxide and quinolinic acid (an endogenous NMDA receptor agonist produced via the kynurenine pathway). Once microglia are "primed" by an acute fluoroquinolone insult, they can remain in a low-grade hyper-reactive state for months or years, explaining why minor secondary stressors—such as a viral upper respiratory infection, psychological stress, sleep deprivation, or environmental chemical exposure (as highlighted in **Yeerkenbieke et al., 2026, *Toxics***, demonstrating synergistic neuroblastoma toxicity between fluoroquinolones and neonicotinoid pesticides)—can trigger disproportionate "neuro-flares."
